<p class="home-lead">Human-AI Interaction: collaboration and trust in complex knowledge tasks.</p>

<div class="home-intro" markdown="1">
I have been a lecturer in the School of Computer Science and Technology at Taiyuan University of Science and Technology since June 2018. I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from Universiti Malaya.

I study **how people and teams can work with AI without losing the ability to verify its output, make their own decisions, or recover when it fails**. I combine empirical human–computer interaction (HCI) with machine learning methods that help measure and control risk under imperfect feedback.

I am first or corresponding author on 23 papers, including <strong class="hl">12 CCF A papers</strong> at CHI (×3), CSCW, UbiComp, ICML, ACL, AAAI, IJCAI, WWW, FSE, and ACM TOIS.
</div>

## Research

<div class="levels">
  <div class="level">
    <span class="level__tag">Individual level</span>
    <h3>How people verify and learn with AI</h3>
    <p>The work of checking AI output, when that work becomes fatiguing, and what learners can still do when the AI is removed.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="level">
    <span class="level__tag">Group level</span>
    <h3>How teams recover when AI breaks down</h3>
    <p>How collaborators notice failures, redistribute work, and keep a shared task moving when AI is embedded in their workflow.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="level">
    <span class="level__tag">Societal level</span>
    <h3>How to evaluate and control AI risk</h3>
    <p>Methods for learning from unevenly sampled feedback and measuring risk across groups and changing conditions.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## Looking ahead

Over the next three to five years, I plan to move from measuring the costs of human-AI collaboration to designing collaboration that people can trust: adapting the roles of people and AI, reducing the effort needed to verify AI output, and evaluating collaboration with statistical guarantees. [Read the research agenda](/research/#research-agenda).

I welcome collaboration on human-AI interaction, AI-assisted programming and education, and trustworthy evaluation of AI systems. Students interested in these topics are welcome to get in touch.
